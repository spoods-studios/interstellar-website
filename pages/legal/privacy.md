This policy explains what Setare Aerospace and this website collect about you, why, and what you can do about it.

## In short

- The game sends nothing to us unless you choose to: a report, a crash bundle, telemetry or the mailing list. You can play without any of them.
- Telemetry and the mailing list stay off until you switch them on, and you can switch them off again just as easily.
- This website counts visits with GoatCounter, which sets no cookies, and the site sets none either.
- We never sell or share your data, and we make no automated decisions about you.
- Ask us to see, correct or delete anything we hold at {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}.

## Who we are

Setare Aerospace and this website are run by {{LEGAL_NAME}} ("we", "us"), the controller of the data this policy covers. Write to us about anything in this policy at {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}.

We have not appointed a data protection officer, because the law does not require one for the small amount of data we handle. Write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} instead.

The game is free and has no accounts. You never sign up with us to play.

## What the game collects by default

Nothing leaves your computer. Your saves, settings, logs, mods and crash bundles stay in folders on your own machine, and the game does not send them anywhere unless you take one of the steps described below.

Each step below is optional. The game plays the same whether you use it or not, and we make no automated decisions about you and build no profile of you from any of it.

## Mods and user content

Mods you install and anything you build in the game stay on your computer. The game does not tell us which mods you use.

When the game's mod browser downloads a mod, the service that hosts the community mods repository handles your IP address to deliver the files, as any download does. We do not receive it.

If you submit a mod to the curated community mods repository, the mod, the name you submit it under and any text you write with it become public in that repository. We use them to review, host and credit your mod. You can ask us to remove your mod at {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}; the licence agreement explains what happens then.

Content you upload to the Steam Workshop is handled by Valve under Steam's own terms and privacy policy, not this one.

## Crash bundles

When the game crashes it writes a crash bundle to a folder on your computer. The bundle stays there. It is sent to us only if you attach it to a report yourself.

A crash bundle can contain file paths on your computer, which may include your username. Look through it before you attach it if that matters to you. Once attached, it is handled exactly like the report it goes with, below.

## Reports and the feedback form

You can send us a bug report or a suggestion from the game, from the [feedback page](../../feedback/) on this website, or with the `/bug` and `/suggest` commands on our Discord server.

- **What we receive:** the text you type and whether it is a bug or a suggestion. From the game we also receive the build version and, only if you tick the box, the last part of the game log or a crash bundle. From Discord we also receive your Discord username, so we can follow up. The website form sends only your text and its kind.
- **Why:** to fix the bug or weigh the idea, and to keep the report service free of spam and abuse.
- **Lawful basis:** our legitimate interest in fixing and improving the game. You can object at any time (see [Your rights](#your-rights)).
- **Who receives it:** a Cloudflare Worker (run for us by Cloudflare) accepts the report and holds it until our own computer collects it and files it as an issue in our self-hosted Forgejo issue tracker, which is not public. Reports sent through Discord pass through Discord first.
- **Abuse protection:** to limit how many reports one sender can make, the Worker keeps a keyed hash of your IP address, never the address itself, and deletes it when the limit's hour or day ends. Cloudflare handles your IP address to deliver the request.
- **How long:** the Worker deletes a report as soon as our tracker has it, usually within minutes. In our tracker we keep the report until its issue closes, then for 12 months.
- **Optional:** you never have to send a report, and play does not depend on it.

Please keep names, passwords, keys and other people's personal data out of what you type or attach. If something like that slips in, write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} and we will remove it.

## Opt-in telemetry

Telemetry is off until you switch it on in the game's settings, and you can switch it off there at any time, as easily as you switched it on.

- **What we receive:** anonymous hardware and session statistics, such as the kind of graphics card and how long sessions last. The version of this policy that comes with telemetry will list every field.
- **Why:** to learn which hardware to support and where the game is slow or crashes.
- **Lawful basis:** your consent, which you can withdraw by switching telemetry off. Withdrawing does not affect what was sent before.
- **Who receives it:** the service that will collect it is named in the version of this policy that comes with telemetry.
- **Identifying?** No. Telemetry carries no name, account or other identifier that ties it to you, so we cannot look up or delete the data of one player.
- **How long:** raw records for at most 12 months; totals that cannot be traced to anyone may be kept longer.
- **Optional:** play works the same with telemetry off.

## The mailing list

You can join our mailing list to hear launch news: the Steam page, the Early Access date and launch day. It uses double opt-in: you only join after you click the link in a confirmation email, and nothing is sent to you before that.

- **What we receive:** your email address, records of when you signed up and confirmed, so we can show you asked to join, and that you ticked the box confirming your age.
- **Why:** to send you the launch news you asked for, and nothing else.
- **Lawful basis:** your consent. Every email has a one-click unsubscribe link, and you can also write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}.
- **Who receives it:** our email provider, Buttondown, a United States company, which stores the list and sends the emails for us. Buttondown keeps the data on Amazon Web Services servers in the US West (Oregon) region, us-west-2. The services Buttondown itself relies on are listed on its [subprocessors page](https://buttondown.com/legal/subprocessors).
- **Transfers:** sending your data from the EU, the EEA or the UK to Buttondown in the United States is protected by the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses (Module 2, controller to processor) with the UK Addendum, under Buttondown's [data processing agreement](https://buttondown.com/legal/data-processing-agreement).
- **Cookies:** this site sets no cookies, the sign-up form included. When you sign up, your browser goes on to Buttondown's own pages to finish, and those follow [Buttondown's privacy policy](https://buttondown.com/legal/privacy).
- **How long:** until you unsubscribe. We keep the record of your consent and of your unsubscribing for up to 3 years after you leave, to show we acted on it, then delete it.
- **Optional:** play and this website work the same without it.

## Site analytics

This website counts visits with GoatCounter, so we can see which pages people read and which links are broken.

- **What GoatCounter receives:** the page you visit, the page that linked you there, your browser and operating system, your screen size and the country your IP address points to. It sets no cookies and stores nothing in your browser, so this site shows no cookie banner. To tell one visit from another, it keeps a hash of your IP address and browser details for a few hours, then discards it; it does not store your IP address.
- **Why:** to keep the site useful and find broken links.
- **Lawful basis:** our legitimate interest in running the website. You can object at any time, and blocking GoatCounter in your browser changes nothing else on the site.
- **Who receives it:** GoatCounter, which runs the counting service for us.
- **Identifying?** No. The counts cannot be traced to you, so we cannot look up or delete the data of one visitor.
- **How long:** the counts are kept as long as the site runs.

The website itself is hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any web host, GitHub handles your IP address to deliver the pages and may keep it in its server logs for security. We do not receive those logs.

## The supporter badge

If you support the game on Patreon, with a tip or by paying for the soundtrack on Bandcamp, you can get a supporter badge: your name in the game's credits, a supporter role on our Discord server and a badge in the game, unlocked by a personal code we send you.

- **What we hold:** the name you want shown in the credits, your Discord username if you want the role, the year of your first contribution, and the code we made for you.
- **Where it comes from:** Patreon or Bandcamp tells us you contributed and gives us the name and contact details you hold with them. Discord gives us your username when you ask for the role.
- **Why:** to credit you, give you the role and make your code.
- **Lawful basis:** your consent. You can withdraw it at any time by writing to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}, and we will take your name out of the next credits update and remove the role.
- **Who receives it:** Discord, to show your role. Your credits name is public in the game.
- **In the game:** the game checks your code on your computer and sends nothing to us when you enter it.
- **How long:** as long as you want to be credited.
- **Optional:** the badge is a thank-you. Nothing in the game depends on it.

## Age rule

You can play at any age. The game collects nothing by default, and schools use it in class.

To send a report, switch on telemetry or join the mailing list you must be 16 or older in the EU and EEA, and 13 or older in the UK and everywhere else.

The game is made for a general audience and is not directed at children under 13. We do not knowingly collect personal data from a child under 13 (COPPA). If we learn that data came from a child under 13, we delete it. A parent or guardian can write to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} to review that data, have it deleted or refuse any further collection.

## Your rights

Under the GDPR and the UK GDPR you have the right to:

- see the data we hold about you and get a copy (access);
- have wrong data corrected (rectification);
- have your data deleted (erasure);
- have us stop using your data for a while (restriction);
- get data you gave us in a form you can take elsewhere (portability);
- object to our use of your data where we rely on legitimate interest (objection);
- withdraw your consent at any time, as easily as you gave it, without affecting what we did before.

Send any request to {{CONTACT_EMAIL}}. It is free. We answer within one month; if a request is complex we may take up to two more months, and we will tell you within the first month if we do. We may ask for proof of identity before we hand over or delete data.

Anonymous telemetry and GoatCounter hashes cannot be linked to you, so we cannot find them to act on a request.

If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, please complain to us first at {{CONTACT_EMAIL}} so we can put it right; in the UK we have to deal with your complaint before you go further. You can always complain to a supervisory authority, such as the one where you live or work. In the UK that is the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO).
